The Decameron

Methodology

By using Boccaccio's Decameron as an exemplar text of the time period, we analyzed the perceptions around the Catholic Church in late Medieval Europe in the wake of the Black Plague. The censorship guidelines used by the church at the time gave us a descriptive list of themes that may have been unsavory and thus banned. We used this guide to flag certain stories in the text as problematic in the eyes of the church, and compare these judgements of our modern sensibilities to actual revisions that were made in the sixteenth century.

Not only does this provide insight into the kind of popular literature in late Medieval Europe, it gives us a sense of the problems people were likely to have with the church. The Decameron was published at a time where significant upheaval was occuring within the Catholic Church, and its publication preceded the Protestant Reformation by only a century. The stories in the Decameron paint a picture of the reputation clergy had come to occupy in the minds of the public: corrupt, hypocritical, and ridiculous. The censorship efforts of two different church officials failed to catch on, and Boccaccio's original remained popular.
