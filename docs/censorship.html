First Day - THE FOURTH STORY

Censorship details for this story coming soon.

Although much of this story was left the same, there are some distinct passages that were subtly changed in order to distance the crude behavior of the Abbot from the moral righteousness of the Catholic Church. One such example of these changes are the passage in paragraphs 2 and 3 which describe the monk discovering the young girl and taking her to his bedchamber. In Boccaccio’s original, the description is relatively neutral and only makes moral comment on the actions in an appropriately ironic tone for the subject matter: “Wherefore, accosting her, he entered into parley with her and so led on from one thing to another that he came to an accord with her and brought her to his cell, unperceived of any” Very obviously, there is a certain disregard for the supposed moral authority of the Clergy, and the Monk is treated as an unfortunately, but not surprisingly, sinful fool. Florio’s censored version from 1620 explicitly moralizes this passage. In a somewhat clumsy manner, a substantial preamble is made to emphasize the aberrant behavior of this particular monk: “Lascivious desire, and no religious devotion, made him draw neere her, and whether under shrift … or some other as close conference, to as pernicious and vile a purpose, I know not: but so farre he prevailed upon her frailety, and such a bargaine passed betweene them, that … he wonne her to his Chamber, before any person could perceive it.” In another section of the story, (paragraph 6) an obvious edit is made to the description of the Abbot’s transgressions. Where Boccaccio provides a (mildly) scandalous description of sexual acts, Florio omits most of the suggestiveness and instead simply says: “ The Maide … was easily wonne to the Abbots will, which caused him to embrace and kisse her often.” This story seems to violate numbers 7 and 8 in the Church’s rules of censorship. Parts of the work are “obscene” or “immoral”, and Florio treats these passages as “isolated heresies” that are then corrected.
